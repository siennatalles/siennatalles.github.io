Problem, approach & results
Problem

48-hour undergraduate data competition at the University of Missouri. SVH's goal was to improve patient experience, so we analyzed how clinical diagnosis, seasonality, and department affect a patient's length of stay in their hospital dataset.

GSOEP Webscraper

Documentation pipeline for 100,000+ variables in a decades-long household survey
PythonRequestsBeautifulSoupMultithreadingPandas
Problem, approach & result
Problem

The German Socio-Economic Panel (SOEP-Core) has tracked the same households since 1984 across 100,000+ variables. Its documentation exists on paneldata.org, but only as individual per-variable pages, not a consolidated reference researchers could search or filter against.

Approach

Built a multithreaded scraper against paneldata.org: pulled the full variable list via its search API, resolved indirect links, tagged each variable's source dataset, and scraped summary statistics, years available, and response scale, with retry logic and checkpointing.

Result

A complete labeling system for the panel, used to prioritize variables for a model predicting personality-based life outcomes at WashU's Personality Measurement and Development Lab.

Melbourne Housing Price Prediction

Comparing four ML models, and figuring out why feature engineering made them worse
With teammate Heidi Schultz
PythonScikit-learnGridSearchCVLinear Regression KNNDecision TreeRandom ForestFeature Engineering
Problem, approach & result
Problem

Predicted median housing prices across 13,580 Melbourne listings, comparing four algorithms end to end instead of settling for the first one that worked.

Approach

Preprocessed with scikit-learn pipelines (imputation, scaling, one-hot encoding), tuned Linear Regression, KNN, Decision Tree, and Random Forest via GridSearchCV, then engineered three new features and retrained all four to compare against the baselines.

Result

Random Forest performed best both before and after feature engineering (R² = 0.83 baseline). The engineered models did worse overall: aggressive feature selection had discarded weaker predictors that were stabilizing the models.

Student Performance Analytics

Catching data leakage and finding what actually predicts student outcomes
With teammates Gabi Wurgaft, Harrison Arons, Ryan Pemberton, and John Collett
RLinear RegressionModel DiagnosticsVIF Model SelectionWLS
Problem, approach & result
Problem

Modeled Overall Score for 10,000 students from a Kaggle dataset, until a suspicious R² of 1 revealed the response variable was leaking into its own predictors.

Approach

Removed the leaking features, ran full diagnostics (variance, normality, leverage, outliers, influence), tested autocorrelation and heteroscedasticity, checked multicollinearity via VIF and condition numbers, and selected a model via exhaustive search and stepwise AIC.

Result

Every method converged on the same two predictors, attendance percentage and internet access, with a lower AIC than the full model.
